Privacy Policy

BetterBookmarks collects no personal data, of any kind, from anyone. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no crash reporting and no server component. This page explains what that means in practice.

Last updated 10 October 2026.

What is stored, and where

Your Spaces, folders, saved pages and settings are stored locally on your Mac, using Safari’s extension storage and IndexedDB. They stay on the device that created them unless you turn on syncing, which is the next section.

Syncing between your Macs

Nothing syncs until you ask it to. When you do, the library is written to your own iCloud as a single file in an app folder in your iCloud Drive. Your account, your storage, your quota. There is no server of mine anywhere in that path and no copy of your library that I could read even if I wanted to.

The file holds what an export holds: Spaces, folders, saved pages and their notes and timestamps. It does not carry your settings, and it does not carry the site-icon cache.

This does not change the section below. The extension still makes no network requests; macOS moves the file, the way it moves any other iCloud document. Stop syncing by not syncing again, and delete the file from iCloud Drive whenever you like.

Network activity

The extension makes no network requests of its own. There is no fetch, no XMLHttpRequest and no WebSocket anywhere in it, no analytics SDK, no remote configuration and no remotely loaded code.

The one thing that touches the network is website icons: each saved page shows its site’s favicon, loaded as an ordinary image directly from that site, with a no-referrer policy. The site sees a request for its own icon and nothing about where it came from. Which icon address worked is remembered locally so the request is not repeated. No third party is involved, and no icon service is used.

Permissions

BetterBookmarks asks for four permissions and no others.

  • storage lets it keep your library and settings on your Mac.
  • tabs lets it tell which of your saved pages are open right now, so that clicking one switches to that tab instead of opening a duplicate.
  • contextMenus lets it add a save item to Safari’s right-click menu.
  • nativeMessaging lets the extension speak to the BetterBookmarks app that contains it, on your Mac. Two things need that and nothing else does: reading and writing the sync file in your own iCloud Drive, which a Safari extension cannot reach by itself, and opening Safari’s settings pane for the extension from a button. It can reach that one app and no other, and nothing in that conversation leaves your Mac.

Website access

Safari puts the address of a tab behind website access, so without it the extension cannot tell which of your saved pages are open, and cannot save the page you are on. That is why it asks, and it asks for nothing beyond that.

BetterBookmarks ships no content scripts. Nothing of it runs inside the pages you visit, so it cannot read, modify or inject anything there, and it has no access to page content, form fields, passwords or browsing history. What it sees is a tab’s address and its title, on your Mac, and it stores neither unless you save the page.

You can grant or withdraw this at any time in Safari → Settings → Extensions → BetterBookmarks.

Import and export

Importing a bookmark file reads that file on your Mac only. Exports are written to a location you choose. Neither operation transmits anything.

Purchases

BetterBookmarks is sold through the Mac App Store. Apple handles the purchase and shares no identifying information about you with me. I see only aggregate sales figures. See Apple’s privacy policy for how Apple handles that transaction.

Children

BetterBookmarks collects no data from anyone, including children under 13. It contains no advertising and no third-party content.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new date. Given that the answer is “nothing is collected”, changes are unlikely.
